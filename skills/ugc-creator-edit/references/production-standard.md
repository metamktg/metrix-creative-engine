# Evergreen production standard

Use this reference with the approved brief, current asset map and the parent UGC workflow. It governs production choices before the five presentation gates. It does not authorize additional deliverables, spend or publication.

Three companion references govern the choices made before footage exists, and the finishing rules below assume they were followed: [script-and-delivery.md](script-and-delivery.md) (spoken register, length as a budget, the per-line delivery map and the `check_script.py` gate), [casting.md](casting.md) (the persona brief from the ICP, identity-still spec, voice as part of the persona, reuse means B-roll not actors) and [lip-sync-routing.md](lip-sync-routing.md) (the three talking-head routes, dated model observations, the outage protocol). [hooks.md](hooks.md) holds the hook patterns, the one-variable hook test and the hook log.

## Plan the sound

Record a music decision for every edit. Select a suitable instrumental bed when it supports the message and creator's delivery. Identify the exact source file and hash, rights evidence, permitted commercial and paid-media uses, restrictions, cue ranges, fades and ducking. Unknown rights block use. A generation result, platform availability or familiar sound is not proof of a commercial license.

An intentional dry treatment requires a specific creative reason and documented reviewer acceptance. Do not silently omit music, add an unlicensed bed, or use music to conceal defective speech. Retain separate voice, music, ambience and effects stems and the final mix recipe.

Let dialogue lead the mix. Duck or arrange the bed so complete words, offer conditions and the destination remain intelligible; avoid pumping, abrupt entrances and clipped endings. Check the final encoded mix on phone speakers, headphones and mono at ordinary and low listening levels. Measure against the approved delivery profile; do not substitute a universal loudness target or fixed music-to-voice ratio for listening evidence.

When music is intended to be audible, verify that it actually registers at ordinary and low playback levels while the voice stays clear. Do not equate a nonzero music stem or a good integrated-loudness reading with an audible, balanced bed. Choose an instrumental passage unless vocals serve an explicit brief purpose; inspect the selected passage for sung words that could compete with the narrator. Give the entrance a deliberate fade or a different source offset so a beat or transient does not collide with the first spoken word. Set the fade, offset, ducking and level from the actual passage and performance, not a universal gain change or fixed fade duration.

Build a coherent narration performance. Where separately generated pickups sound detached, prefer a continuous accepted read or repair only the affected phrases while preserving the approved voice. Compare phrase entrances, emphasis, pitch peaks, timbre and loudness before the final mix; normalize or ride phrases only as needed, keeping expressive intent. Do not flatten intended emphasis or accelerate speech to hide a mismatch. If the result sounds like two voices, listen to isolated stems and inspect timeline overlap, retained source audio, doubled pickups, vocal music and room/delay effects. One encoded audio stream can contain several competing voices, and final mix normalization cannot diagnose that. Record the cause, affected ranges and the narrow repair.

Define a credible recording and microphone perspective. Keep the same acoustic profile through off-camera footage, product material, graphics and the CTA. A picture change does not create a new recording event. Establish a visible speaking-setting change before changing its profile. Preserve word tails, compatible level and natural room decay. Avoid artificial hiss, breaths, damage or reflections added solely to manufacture realism.

## Let the message determine the duration

Write a concise thought-led performance with one intended action. The brief's maximum duration is a ceiling, not a slot to fill. Remove repeated setup and redundant explanations before extending the edit or accelerating speech. Preserve meaningful emphasis, natural variation, complete words and a comfortable close.

Speech speed-up is not the default pacing tool. First use useful shot lengths, silence trims that preserve articulation, clear reveals, reframing and relevant cutaways. Keep visible speech and its audio synchronized. Record source and output ranges, speed factor and processing method for every retimed span. Review the result at normal playback speed; a technically valid time map does not prove natural delivery.

Retime silent footage or navigation only when gestures, motion and the demonstrated process remain credible and truthful. Settle into a readable hold for evidence. Do not imply a faster process, omit a necessary step or conceal a defect through speed changes. Export for normal playback and inspect that export rather than relying on an accelerated review preview.

## Give each cut a purpose

Every visual change should help the viewer recognize the situation, understand a thought, inspect evidence, resolve uncertainty or act. Use direct cuts, audio bridges, match cuts and transitions where they serve that purpose. Do not impose a cut-frequency quota, synchronize every sentence to music, or add an effect merely to make the edit appear more produced.

Check entry and exit frames, word tails, visual continuity and caption changes at each cut. Give useful details time to register. Keep captions in complete thought groups, within the placement's safe regions and clear of faces and evidence. Avoid orphan words, duplicate competing overlays and abrupt changes of text hierarchy.

Choose a deliberate, format-appropriate proof treatment from the brief and any bound accepted reference. Real interaction capture, genuine UI layers/composites, restrained motion, reveals, annotations and readable static proof are available methods, not a mandatory template. Do not turn a product demonstration into an automatic screenshot slideshow, impose a motion quota or generalize one project's preferred layout into an engine default. Time a crop, reveal, highlight or hold to the corresponding spoken feature/action, with enough reading time and no disconnected static beat. A local repair should preserve unrelated accepted treatment decisions.

At any UI/composite handoff, inspect the actual transition frames for two offset copies, double images, ghosts, crowding and caption collisions. Choose a purposeful clean transition when a dissolve produces a doubled screen. Test both ends and continuous playback; a settled contact sheet can miss the defect. Do not fabricate interactive controls, clicks, answers or product states in the animation.

Match gaze, expression and body action to the spoken thought. Check the transition from addressing camera to working, not only the first and last pose. Trim an awkward look, use a clean action range or a relevant cutaway before paying for regeneration. Do not introduce a fixed glance duration or a universal smile requirement. Any reaction, gesture or angle change must feel plausible at the original normal speech rate.

## Accept B-roll before it reaches the timeline

Choose footage for relevance and believable usable action before resolution or model prestige. For each selected range, record its purpose, source hash, rights, trim handles and acceptance evidence. Check:

- Native detail after the actual delivery crop, with adequate focus, exposure and legibility.
- Credible motion cadence, stable objects and backgrounds, and no distracting looping, morphing or interpolation.
- Anatomical plausibility, hands, grips, props and any text that the viewer can inspect.
- Required subject, product, wardrobe, lighting and setting continuity.
- Consistency with the spoken meaning and supported claims, without implying an unverified result or personal experience.

Inspect the selected range and the actual encoded output at normal speed. Examine critical motion and cut frames closely; a good still is not motion acceptance. Use higher-quality existing ranges when they meet the purpose. Upscaling can improve presentation detail but cannot repair anatomy, distorted lettering, temporal drift, continuity or relevance.

When a new presenter is requested, retain suitable approved B-roll, product captures, graphics and editable sources byte for byte as library originals. Give any new crop or retimed derivative its own path and hash. Separate reusable non-identifying footage from footage containing the prior presenter's face, speaking mouth, distinctive wardrobe or voice. Inspect the exact proposed ranges for continuity; do not silently reuse the prior identity or its voice/listening approval for the new presenter. A new identity/voice needs its own acceptance while unrelated accepted product material remains available.

Preserve what a source actually proves. A genuine feature illustration can show the interface vocabulary without proving a complete interaction, a live state, a result or the answer to a question. Bind the narration to the captured evidence. If the brief requires a full product flow and only generic cards exist, record the missing capture rather than promoting those cards into proof or generating a substitute interface.

## Finish with one clear action

Carry a consistent intended action, accurate destination and necessary offer conditions into the ending. Establish the CTA while it is being spoken when that improves comprehension. Preserve its proportions and typography; measure text fit instead of stretching it. Keep the card or equivalent action treatment stable long enough to read, preserve the complete final word and recording tail, and inspect the final frame. Avoid competing closing treatments that obscure which action matters.

Editing choices are hypotheses about attention, comprehension and response. Do not label them proven CRO, hook-rate, retention or conversion improvements without applicable delivery evidence.

## Execution and acceptance records

Extend the shot plan with these columns:

`Timecode | Viewer question/purpose | Visual/action/framing | Source hash/ranges | Output ranges/speed | On-camera status/acoustic profile | Transition/GFX | Music/ambience/SFX cue and rights | Acceptance evidence`

Retain the music decision and rights evidence, stems and mix recipe; source/crop and speed maps; selected B-roll acceptance evidence; current transcript and captions; actual-export technical measurements; normal-speed playback and listening records; and a concise timecoded defect/review log. Bind reviews to the exact current export and relevant sources. A later edit invalidates affected acceptance and requires the appropriate re-review.

Record the brief-specific editing criteria with timecode, status and evidence: narration coherence/phrase consistency, competing-voice diagnosis when applicable, music audibility and entrance, matching product/spoken beats, UI/composite continuity, gaze/action continuity and readable proof/CTA holds. Mark each decision's scope as engine-wide, brand/project or this run, and bind accepted style references by path/hash where used. A project preference does not become a shared default. These are review criteria within the bound profile and editorial evidence, not invented provider parameters or a claim that an automatic detector judged them.

## Preserve a recoverable production state

Before clearing superseded drafts, distinguish accepted library originals, editable projects, linked media, stems, rights, job/cost ledgers, review evidence and disposable render intermediates. Build a recovery manifest of the material to preserve with contained paths, file sizes and hashes for retained critical sources. Verify the destination copy/archive can be read and that the editable recipe can resolve its media. Check working and archive free space before creating another full package or render. A failed or partial archive is not authorization to remove its source.

Use the user's authorized cleanup scope and retain an explicit restore/resume entry point. Moving recoverable drafts out of the active folder is different from permanently deleting sources. Never delete accepted originals, unrelated user work, dependencies or inaccessible paths merely because disk space is low. State any unreadable or unverified archive ranges honestly. Provider job recovery alone is not a production archive.

Use these artifacts at the mandatory gates:

| Gate ID | Production evidence it needs |
| --- | --- |
| `iap_variables` | Brief and variable fidelity, technical delivery profile, source/crop/speed maps, sync, text fit, rights and actual-export measurements. |
| `communications` | Clear opening, concise supported message, useful evidence, coherent sequence, one intended action and accurate conditions. |
| `sound_check` | Actual final audio comprehension, intelligible complete words and destination, plain-language understanding without production explanation, and accessible captions. |
| `editorial_peer` | Actual encoded playback, listening and close inspection of cuts, B-roll, mix, captions and ending; specific defects resolved against the current files. |
| `blind_review` | The current neutral review copy and necessary de-identified context, without prior scores or the producer's rationale; an objective assessment of comprehension, distraction and action. |

These are evidence requirements, not automatic passes. Record the actual reviewer, method and limits. Automated measurements, frame inspection and another agent's assessment do not establish human listening or playback acceptance. Missing rights or required review evidence remains unresolved; do not fabricate approval to present an asset as ready.
