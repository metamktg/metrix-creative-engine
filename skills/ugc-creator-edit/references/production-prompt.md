# Creator-led UGC: production and editing direction

**Understand the person and the objective.** Identify the audience's situation, the speaker's plausible relationship to them, the promise supported by the brief, the product mechanism and one intended action. Preserve claims and offer conditions. Do not invent credentials, personal experience, results, comments or social proof. Each beat must resolve a question, demonstrate something, reassure or invite action.

**Create pace in the edit.** Pacing means how the finished video progresses, not consistently fast talking. Use shot lengths, frame-level trims, selective footage playback-speed changes, reframing, reveals, audio bridges and contrasts between quick transitions and useful holds. Keep vocal cadence conversational and meaning-led. Do not speed up speech as an automatic shortcut. Retime B-roll or screen navigation only when the action still looks credible; return to a readable hold for proof. Preserve the truth of the demonstrated process. Keep visible speech and its audio together. Never sacrifice complete words, intelligibility, natural gestures or CTA reading time to hit a beat.

**Build a credible recording world.** Choose the setting from the brief: home desk, office, outdoors, gym, car or podcast setup only when it suits the speaker and message. Define the visible recording method, plausible microphone distance and acoustic profile. Additional locations need a narrative reason, not a variety quota. An intimate podcast sound can fit a visible close-mic setup; other settings need their own believable perspective. Keep clear vocal detail and modest, appropriate room presence. Avoid hiss, fake microphone damage, excessive reverb, artificial breaths and processing that manufactures imperfections.

**Preserve acoustic continuity.** Maintain the main creator's exact acoustic profile through B-roll, product screens, graphics and CTA cards. Off-camera narration remains the same speaking event. Change the profile only when the creator is visibly established speaking in a new setting. Make that handoff quick and comfortable at a suitable phrase boundary, preserving word tails, level continuity and short room decay. A visual transition or SFX must not hide an audio defect.

**Keep the performance and mix coherent.** Compare phrase entrances, level, timbre and emphasis. Use a continuous accepted read when pickups sound disconnected, or repair only the affected phrases. Diagnose apparent doubled speech by isolating narration, source audio, music vocals and room/delay effects; a single encoded stream proves nothing about competing voices. Keep intended music audible below clear speech at ordinary and low playback levels, use a passage without competing lyrics, and choose a gentle entrance/source offset that does not strike against the opening word. Preserve exact stems and processing evidence. No universal gain, fade or loudness number replaces listening.

**Earn attention from the opening through the CTA.** In the first three seconds, establish a recognizable tension, intriguing observation or useful visual contradiction with a clear payoff. A specific relevant hook is preferable to indiscriminate loudness or flashing effects. Vary framing and media as the thought changes: credible creator shot, contextual action, actual product detail, demonstration and invitation. Preserve room for the useful idea to register. Do not leave the avatar in an identical talking frame throughout, or replace it with a tiny floating face on empty slides.

**Use CapCut-style craft selectively.** Consider direct and match cuts, a brief whip/reframe, page movement, a clean green-screen cutout, a selective sticker, an underline or a restrained punch-in. Choose according to the action and viewer's next need. Glitches, memes, reaction frames, kick hits and soundboard cues are optional tools, never a checklist. Match a chosen effect's peak to the relevant gesture, reveal or audio transient at frame precision when that improves the moment. Do not force every sentence or cut onto a music beat. Keep captions readable, phrase-led, inside placement-safe regions and clear of faces/product evidence. Avoid competing duplicate overlays.

**Choose a purposeful proof treatment.** Bind the approved project/run reference rather than generalizing it into an engine template. Choose real interaction capture, genuine UI layers/composites, motion/reveals/annotations or a readable static hold when they serve the format and spoken feature. Do not default to a screenshot slideshow, invent interface behavior or require motion everywhere. Preserve unrelated accepted choices during a local repair. Inspect transitions continuously for double images, ghosts and collisions. Match gaze and expression to the thought, and use a believable look-to-work action range. Repair an awkward glance or overlong beat through editing before regeneration. Record engine-wide versus brand/project versus run-specific decision scope.

**Verify current references.** Inspect a small, relevant set of recent examples for the intended platform and audience. Record source, date, specific technique and usage rights. Use a trend only if it fits the brief and retains the message. Do not call an unverified effect viral, assume a platform sound is licensed for another platform's paid ads, or claim an editing tactic objectively improves CVR without client test evidence. Test retention alongside qualified clicks and the actual conversion objective.

**Use deliberate review passes.** First assemble the clearest rough cut. Then reassess it as a skeptical viewer: what feels too polished, repetitive, confusing, distracting or slow? Finally refine the specific moments that weaken recognition, proof or action. These are editorial perspective changes, not instructions to fabricate mistakes, simulate a diagnosis, invent colleague feedback or claim hours of human work. Keep only a concise decision and defect log.

**Spend on missing material, not exploration by generation.** Inventory usable library footage and real product captures before requesting shots. Give each missing shot a purpose, action, framing, continuity requirements, usable duration and trim handles. Approve the full vocal performance before lip sync. Keep recoverable voice, music, ambience and SFX stems. Build one low-cost motion proof where direction is uncertain and accepted material cannot answer it. Repair by editing when possible; request a new generation only for an identified material defect or gap, with a current cost bound and capacity left for finishing. Preserve successful sources and avoid blind retries. An unknown submission retains its reserved cost until resolved.

**Change the presenter without discarding the library.** Retain fitting approved B-roll, real product captures and graphics byte for byte, with exact hashes, rights and usable ranges. Review old-presenter faces, lips, voice and identity cues separately; their acceptance does not transfer to a new performer. Give derivatives new paths. Do not pretend a generic feature card proves a complete live interaction or generate UI to fill missing evidence. Before authorized draft cleanup, verify a recoverable archive/manifest and editable relinking, preserve critical originals and ledgers, and check disk capacity. An unfinished archive or inaccessible path remains unresolved.

**Close clearly and review honestly.** Match the CTA to the brief's real destination and offer. For a digital service, normally use an integrated action card or equally explicit ending. Hold it long enough to understand, preserve the final spoken word, and remove any silent apparent speech. Review raw voice and final mix through actual human listening. Review the full edit at normal speed, muted at phone size and in its intended placement. Check every cut, visible mouth span, setting handoff and closing frame. A technical check is not a human acceptance verdict.
