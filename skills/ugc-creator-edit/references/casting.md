# Casting the presenter

Casting binds the approved message to a credible speaker, voice and recording situation. It is a brief-specific production decision, not an attractiveness, age, accent, wardrobe or selfie template. Use assigned cast when suitable; replace the presenter when the brief or current user instruction requires it. A new creative does not automatically require a new face.

A replacement creates new identity, voice and performance acceptance. Retain suitable accepted B-roll, genuine UI, graphics and music under their source/rights scope. Old face, lips, distinctive identity cues and voice approval do not transfer. See [preproduction-director.md](preproduction-director.md) for the dependency handoff.

## 1. Establish the speaker's relationship

Define why this person can address this viewer here. Separate a fictional demonstrator, an educator, a real creator and an actual customer. A synthetic actor can explain supported mechanisms without inventing personal use or outcomes. An authentic personal-use claim needs the actual speaker's supported experience and permission; role or disclosure alone cannot establish that evidence.

Use observable traits from the approved brief and audience situation. Do not infer competence, parenthood, product use, vocal register or emotional style from demographics. No named real people, public figures or lookalikes in generated casting. Keep completed casting records private.

## 2. One portable performer card

Record these choices once in the production/profile record and reference them from script and shots:

| Decision | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Role and POV | Speaker relationship, allowed claim scope and intended viewer. |
| Identity | Presenter ID, actual reference file/hash, observable look and current acceptance scope. |
| Situation | Why this person is here, what they are doing and why they address the viewer now. |
| Voice | Actual voice identity or supported reference, model/settings, register, cadence, pronunciation, emotional range and listening observations. |
| Scene | Wardrobe, staging, camera/framing, microphone distance, room/acoustic profile, posture, eyeline, hand/prop capacity and continuity anchors. |
| Fit decision | Relevant alternatives when unresolved, selected fit, evidence, rejected alternatives and limits. |

Describe voice fit in practical listening terms. A wide shot with unexplained intimate studio audio can fail the recording illusion; deliberate stylization remains possible when the brief calls for it. Appearance alone does not predict voice. Provider actor/voice IDs belong in adapter bindings so the human-readable card survives route changes.

## 3. Identity and motion inputs

Reuse a current accepted identity reference when authorized. Resolve uncertainty with the smallest useful preview before dependent expensive work. Frame, expression, headroom, lower-face visibility, lighting and dimensions must fit the selected operation. A lip-sync still needs a usable visible mouth, not one universal camera specification.

Inspect the actual still for the brief's traits, identity, scene, unwanted text/logos and framing. A still cannot approve motion, voice or lip sync. Additional views need supported inputs and a concrete reason; no fixed model, resolution, seed availability or view count governs every run.

Carry actual files through supported reference inputs. Text repetition can reinforce continuity but cannot guarantee pixels. Distinguish identity, style, performance, product and exact UI proof references. A failed identity upload stops or repairs that binding; it does not authorize a replacement face.

## 4. Audition voice and scene together

Reuse valid accepted voice when held fixed. When voice is unresolved, compare only relevant candidates inside authorization. Preview actual audio, then accept the exact complete vocal performance before dependent lip sync under the approved-audio route. A short sample establishes limited fit, not whole-script naturalness.

Keep the actual voice/settings contract for retakes. Stability, similarity, style, speed and emotion controls are model-dependent; current supported settings and actual listening govern. Do not universally lower stability, raise pitch or add imperfections. Preserve a coherent accepted body read rather than unrelated phrases with different acoustic perspectives.

Bind identity, script, voice/model/settings, scene and complete audio file/hash to reviewer, access method, scope, decision, timecoded defects and limits. Material binding changes make affected acceptance stale. If the reviewer cannot hear the file, listening remains pending or unavailable.

## 5. Performance proof and continuity

Where required, use a representative motion proof from the intended route to check identity, mouth timing, gaze, gesture and room/camera coherence. Reuse passing footage when suitable. A proof is not full-export acceptance.

Gaze follows the thought and actual task. Direct address may sustain comfortable lens contact; working footage may attend to the work. A route needing stable visible lips can use B-roll for other angles; that constraint stays in its profile. Do not force a smile, hair touch, stumble or camera move to imitate humanity.

Specify a feasible action and explicit hand/prop capacity per shot. Requested affect and observed delivery are separate facts. Review actual behavior against its communication purpose.

## 6. Replacement, comparisons and rights

Retain original accepted source hashes, rights, usable ranges and destinations. Give crops/composites separate paths/hashes. Identity-bearing old B-roll needs an explicit continuity or non-identifying treatment decision. Recheck timing/readability in the new edit without regenerating unchanged truthful sources.

In a live matrix the assigned presenter, identity and relevant settings stay fixed unless presenter is the declared variable. Additional variants require authorization and their own acceptance. No demographic or preview predicts resonance.

Keep talent/source rights, generated provenance and placement-specific disclosure decisions private. Verify current launch requirements through the launch owner. Casting documentation grants no spend or publication authority, and a model job cannot replace the five final reviews.
